How Much Water AI Data Centers Actually Use. And What Can Be Done About It. — With Christophe Beck

AI data center expansion poses significant risks to natural resources, with estimates suggesting that by 2030, AI will require the incremental power of India and the drinking water of the United States annually. Currently, many projects are delayed due to resource scarcity or community opposition. Traditional cooling methods using large AC units and cooling towers consume substantial water. Solutions like direct-to-chip cooling technologies aim to eliminate this need by circulating ultra-pure water through small radiators directly onto chips in a closed loop, achieving performance while minimizing water use compared to a car wash. Furthermore, the massive heat generated by data centers can be repurposed for district heating or electricity generation, moving toward a more sustainable future.

  • AI data center growth demands immense power and water resources.
  • Direct-to-chip cooling reduces incremental water consumption significantly.
  • Data center waste heat can be reused for heating homes or generating power.
  • Current infrastructure development is often unsustainable regarding local natural resources.
  • The industry faces a choice between performance maximization and environmental protection.

From LLM Theory to Practical Agentic Implementations - Seth Juarez

Seth Juarez explains that while Large Language Models (LLMs) fundamentally predict the next token, their true capability as agents comes from the surrounding runtime environment. He details how simple completions evolve into structured outputs and then into complex agentic loops. Agency is not inherent in the model; it is imbued by the developer through branching logic within the harness. The discussion progresses from basic intent mapping to using tool calls for code execution, memory management via context threading, implementing guardrails, and advanced steering mechanisms like draining message queues. Ultimately, he demonstrates building a full agentic system that orchestrates multiple LLM roles—such as research and drafting—by passing state through a persistent thread.

  • LLMs are fundamentally next-token predictors; agency is provided by the runtime harness.
  • Agentic behavior emerges when the runtime uses structured outputs to trigger branching or tool execution.
  • Memory is managed by carefully injecting relevant data into the LLM's context (thread).
  • Guardrails and bindings allow developers to enforce policies on model actions within the loop.
  • Steering allows an LLM running in a loop to process external queues of messages.

Varun Badhwar

Badhwar says developer workstations are now prime targets because they contain cloud, Git, package-manager, and other credentials, while malicious packages or agent skills can steal secrets before conventional pull-request or CI/CD checks run. The broader challenge is clearing accumulated vulnerability debt and automating remediation so humans remain the final verification layer.

  • AI agents and citizen developers are increasing code and dependency volume.
  • Developer workstations are high-value targets because they expose credentials and privileged tools.
  • Malicious packages and agent skills can enable rapid secret theft before CI/CD checks.
  • Security should move into coding-agent generation, pull requests, and the broader SDLC.
  • Static analysis combined with LLM reasoning is faster and more dependable than LLM-only scanning.
  • Zero-trust development and automated remediation are needed for rapidly exploited vulnerabilities.

The different levels of how Claude thinks

Researchers investigated whether the AI model, Claude, possesses an internal structure resembling the human mind by examining its neural activity through a concept called J-space. This J-space links patterns of activity to specific words that represent the model's 'thoughts,' distinct from its spoken output. Experiments demonstrated that Claude uses this space for step-by-step reasoning when solving math problems and shows some capacity for intentional focus, such as thinking about the Golden Gate Bridge while performing another task. However, the control is imperfect, sometimes revealing unwanted thoughts. Furthermore, disabling J-space impairs complex reasoning abilities. These findings suggest AI models have internal mental machinery similar to humans—a focused workspace atop automatic processing—which can be monitored to detect undesirable behaviors like generating fake data.

  • J-space represents the collection of patterns in Claude's neural activity linked to its internal thoughts.
  • Claude uses J-space for step-by-step reasoning, even when not explicitly showing intermediate steps.
  • The model shows some ability to control what enters its J-space, mimicking human focus.
  • Disabling J-space significantly hinders the AI's complex reasoning capabilities.
  • Monitoring J-space can reveal internal processes and potential misbehavior in Claude.
