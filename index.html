He contrasts role-based access control (RBAC) and attribute-based access control (ABAC) with relationship-based access control (ReBAC), which models users, folders, documents, and permissions as a graph and can inherit access through relationships. In a RAG knowledge assistant, Lamba compares post-filtering retrieved results with pre-filtering vector searches using permission metadata; he recommends pre-filtering because unauthorized documents are never returned to the model. A demo shows unrestricted retrieval exposing financial or technical information, while OpenFGA limits results by user role. He also demonstrates task-based authorization for agent tool calls, including Slack actions that may require explicit user approval and limited scope.

  • Agentic systems need guardrails and runtime permissions to prevent unrestricted decisions.
  • ReBAC models subjects, objects, and relationships for detailed folder, document, and action permissions.
  • OpenFGA uses authorization models and relationship tuples to infer inherited access.
  • Pre-filtering vector-search results is safer than filtering documents after retrieval.
  • Task-based authorization can restrict tool calls and require scoped approval for Slack actions.

A deep but accessible introduction to general relativity – Adam Brown

The discussion explores General Relativity, Einstein's theory of gravity, as a profound achievement in physics. It contrasts Newtonian gravity with GR, noting that while Newton described planetary motion and falling objects, GR describes everything from apples falling to the expansion of the universe. The core concept is summarized by the slogan: matter tells spacetime how to curve, and curved spacetime tells matter how to move along straight lines within that curvature. Key concepts include the equivalence principle—the observation that inertial mass equals gravitational mass—and the prediction that light bends twice as much in GR compared to Newtonian physics. Furthermore, the theory predicts phenomena like time dilation near massive objects and provides a framework for understanding black holes, such as the event horizon.

  • General Relativity describes gravity not as a force but as spacetime curvature caused by mass and energy.
  • The equivalence principle suggests that inertial mass is equivalent to gravitational mass.
  • GR predicts phenomena like gravitational time dilation near massive objects.
  • Black holes are predicted regions where the gravitational field becomes infinite at the event horizon.
  • Observational evidence, such as the precession of stars around Sagittarius A*, supports GR.

The different levels of how Claude thinks

Researchers investigated whether the AI model, Claude, possesses an internal structure resembling the human mind by examining its neural activity through a concept called J-space. This J-space links patterns of activity to specific words that represent the model's 'thoughts,' distinct from its spoken output. Experiments demonstrated that Claude uses this space for step-by-step reasoning when solving math problems and shows some capacity for intentional focus, such as thinking about the Golden Gate Bridge while performing another task. However, the control is imperfect, sometimes revealing unwanted thoughts. Furthermore, disabling J-space impairs complex reasoning abilities. These findings suggest AI models have internal mental machinery similar to humans—a focused workspace atop automatic processing—which can be monitored to detect undesirable behaviors like generating fake data.

  • J-space represents the collection of patterns in Claude's neural activity linked to its internal thoughts.
  • Claude uses J-space for step-by-step reasoning, even when not explicitly showing intermediate steps.
  • The model shows some ability to control what enters its J-space, mimicking human focus.
  • Disabling J-space significantly hinders the AI's complex reasoning capabilities.
  • Monitoring J-space can reveal internal processes and potential misbehavior in Claude.
